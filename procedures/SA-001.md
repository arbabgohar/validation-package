# Qaabil supplier assessment procedure

SA-001, Rev A (draft), October 2026. Referenced by VP-001 and RA-001. Items in \[brackets\] are open.

## Purpose

This procedure defines how Qaabil chooses, assesses, approves, and monitors the outside services it depends on, so that customers' quality records are not put at risk by a supplier. Qaabil relies on suppliers for hosting, sign-in, email, and the AI model, and a weakness in any of them becomes a weakness in Qaabil.

## Scope

Any external service or component that stores, processes, or transmits customer data, or that the running of Qaabil depends on. Open-source code libraries are handled by dependency scanning under SEC-007 and CC-001, and are outside this procedure unless they carry customer data.

## Roles

- **Supplier owner:** \[name\]. Runs assessments and keeps the supplier register.

- **Independent reviewer:** \[name\]. Reviews assessments of critical suppliers.

## Criticality

| Level | Meaning | Examples |
|----|----|----|
| Critical | Holds or processes customer records, or an outage stops Qaabil from working | Cloud host, identity provider, AI model provider, inbound email service |
| Important | Supports development, operation, or monitoring but does not hold customer records | Source control and build service, error monitoring |
| Minor | Convenience tools | Design and documentation tools |

## What is assessed

For critical suppliers, before first use:

- **Security assurance:** current independent reports or certifications, such as SOC 2 or ISO 27001, and how they are shared

- **Data location:** where data is stored and processed, which must be in the United States for v1

- **Data use and retention:** whether customer data is used to train models or for other purposes, how long it is kept, and whether retention can be limited or switched off

- **Subprocessors:** who else handles the data, and how changes are announced

- **Reliability:** availability commitments, status reporting, and recovery arrangements

- **Incident notification:** the supplier's duty and timing to tell Qaabil about a security incident

- **Change notice:** how much warning there is before features, versions, or models are retired or changed

- **Contract terms:** data processing terms, liability, and exit and data return arrangements

- **Fit with Qaabil's controls:** for example, whether the identity provider supports multi-factor authentication and fresh re-authentication, and whether the model provider lets Qaabil pin a model version

Important suppliers receive a shorter review. Minor suppliers are recorded only.

## Procedure

1.  **Identify the need** and list candidate suppliers.

2.  **Assess** against the criteria above, using the supplier's own documents and written answers, and record what was checked and what is missing.

3.  **Decide.** Approve, approve with conditions, or reject. Conditions might include a particular plan tier or a configuration setting. Critical decisions are reviewed by the independent reviewer.

4.  **Enter in the supplier register.**

5.  **Configure** the service to meet the conditions, and record the settings.

6.  **Monitor.** Review supplier notices and status pages, subprocessor changes, and incidents. Re-assess critical suppliers at least once a year and whenever a supplier announces a material change.

7.  **Handle supplier changes.** Changes such as a retired model version, a new subprocessor, or a changed API follow CC-001. A change to the AI model or its terms is always a Class 1 change.

8.  **Exit.** Keep a note of how to move to another supplier and how to retrieve data.

## Supplier register

For each supplier the register holds: name, service, criticality, data held, data location, assessment date, decision and conditions, contract and data processing terms, key settings, next review date, and contact.
